# Script skill: how every story must be built (owner, 2026-11-26: "be strict; a weak plot must never be made")

`pipeline/writer.py` puts this whole file into the writer's prompt. `pipeline/story_gate.py` then checks every draft by code,
and a separate "story editor" scores it. A draft that fails is rewritten. If no draft passes, **no video is made**.

## 1. Plan before writing (the "story" block)
- **theme_rule:** ONE sentence, the only magic or law in the story. It must be understood in 3 seconds and can be SEEN.
- **logline:** who wants what, what stands in the way, what they lose if they fail. One sentence.
- **characters:** 2 to 4, from the cast list. Each has a **role** (hero, friend, rival, mayor...), a **want** (a concrete thing
  they are trying to get or protect) and a **personality** (2-4 words). Every character must DO something that changes the story.
- **clue:** a small, ordinary-looking detail said in one of lines 2-5.
- **twist:** uses that exact clue, changes what the beginning meant, and answers a question the viewer already has.
- **ending:** what is different now for the hero.

## 2. Beats, in this order (every line gets one "beat")
`hook` (line 1) → `setup` → `goal` → `consequence` → `escalation` (2 or more) → `choice` → `twist` → `explanation` →
`resolution` (2 or more) → `ending`.
- hook: a named person + one concrete impossible fact + an unanswered question.
- goal: say plainly what the hero wants and what they could lose.
- every consequence and escalation is CAUSED by the theme_rule or by the hero's own choice. Join lines with "so" or "but".
- choice: the hero decides something hard. The hero solves the problem; luck or an adult out of nowhere never does.
- twist and explanation: say the truth in plain words, then why it happened.

## 3. Every line is a scene (be specific)
- **scene:** one clear sentence of what we SEE: who, where, doing what.
- **emotions:** the feeling of EVERY character on screen, by name. Feelings change as the story changes; two people in the same
  shot usually feel different things. Do not use "neutral" for more than 2 lines.
- **shot:** place, action and camera that show exactly what the line says. If it cannot be shown with the listed places,
  actions, faces, monster form and props, rewrite the line.
- The animation cannot show small hand-held things (bags, hats, keys, photos, letters). Never make the story depend on one,
  unless that line's prop is a sign or screen that names it.

## 4. Automatic rejection (never write these)
- A second rule, power or curse added later.
- A coincidence, a dream, "it was all fake", or a twist with no clue.
- A twist that contradicts an earlier line, or a fact that makes no sense (an empty bag that is heavy).
- A moral or lesson as the ending; a "Before you..." or "Nobody tells you..." opener.
- The narrator switching to "I" or "my" outside a short quoted line.
- A hero who only watches. Characters who appear and never matter.
- A premise close to a recent title, or copied from another creator.
- Violence, death, weapons, bullying that is rewarded, or anything frightening for children. Monsters never hurt anyone.

## 5. Self-check before answering
Retell the story in 3 sentences. If you cannot, or if a 10-year-old would ask "but why?", fix it before you answer.
