# TeamDeam #4 Short, analysis (checked 2026-11-22)

**Video:** "In This Town, Everyone Always Need To Wear A Halloween Costume..." (https://www.youtube.com/shorts/4romFqTXv-I)
**Views:** 4.9M · **Length:** about 178 s (nearly 3 minutes, not a typical short) · **About 480 words** · **Music:** a soft bed with swells at the turns (the captions mark [music] at about 15 points).

## Beat structure (paraphrased)
1. **0–6 s, rule hook:** everyone in the town must wear a Halloween costume everywhere: school, dinner, even sleep.
2. **6–16 s, world:** each person has a different costume, but one rule nobody breaks: you never take it off.
3. **16–25 s, the narrator's doubt:** as a child he asked his mum why, and she covered his mouth and told him never to ask again.
4. **25–45 s, the demonstration:** his friend Daniel pulls off his pirate hat at lunch and laughs that the rule is stupid. The cafeteria freezes and a teacher screams.
5. **45–60 s, the consequence:** Daniel is gone, the costume stands up by itself, and everyone, even his parents, claims he never existed. Only the narrator remembers.
6. **60–90 s, the mystery:** the narrator notices costumes that look too old. A new girl arrives in a plain costume with no zipper, and she refuses to tell her name.
7. **90–110 s, the warning:** she grabs his arm and asks if he has ever seen his parents without theirs. He can't remember.
8. **110–130 s, the night scare:** he shines a flashlight on his dad, something moves underneath the costume, and his dad is standing behind him.
9. **130–160 s, the truth:** at an old costume shop, there are photos hundreds of years old, including one of the narrator. The costumes hold something underneath that tries to escape every Halloween.
10. **160–178 s, the cliffhanger:** his own fingers start to look strange, his father's voice says not to listen, and his reflection reaches for its own zipper.

## How it is told
- **Voice:** first person, calm and a little scared, like telling a secret. The pace speeds up during the chase and slows down for the reveals. Each section ends on a small hook.
- **Emotion map:** curious → uneasy → sudden shock (the cafeteria) → grief and loneliness (only he remembers) → suspicion → fear → awe → dread.
- **Visuals:** blocky avatars in costumes, close single shots with the costume as the focus, the school cafeteria, a bedroom at night with a flashlight, and a dark costume shop.
- **Music:** gentle with swells. We do not use their track.

## Why it works
- The rule is clear in 5 s and the consequence is shown, not told.
- The "only I remember" idea gives it a personal stake.
- A new character with a secret (the new girl) keeps a question open.
- Every 20–30 s something changes (a vanishing, a stranger, a night scare, a reveal).
- It ends on an unresolved dread, which gets re-watches and comments.

## What we kept, what we added (episode 7)
- **Kept:** the rule world, the friend who breaks the rule, the vanishing, only the narrator remembering, the new girl, the parents question, a night scare, and a dread ending.
- **Changed:** a white smile mask instead of costumes, new names and lines, and a much shorter runtime (about 65 s).
- **Added for hook, twist and plot:**
  1. **Title hook with the consequence:** "taking off your mask erases you from everyone's memory", so the stakes are in the first line.
  2. **A planted clue:** Rex scratches his mask when nervous (early). The new girl scratches hers the same way, so the viewer can guess the twist.
  3. **A reveal that pays off the clue:** the new girl is Rex, back with a new face that nobody ever looks at.
  4. **A stronger personal stake:** the mask is what keeps you remembered, and the narrator has no one else.
  5. **A last twist and loop:** the narrator's own mask starts to itch. He is next, and the last line sends viewers back to the first.
