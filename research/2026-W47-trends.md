## Structure map (from what could be observed)
- **Zack D. Films:** one question in the first second → answer → hard cut when curiosity ends. No outro, and the title acts as the thumbnail.
- **Stickman habit Short (BillionInspire):** a number plus a "you" promise ("5 minutes") → rule/mechanism → payoff. Same visual language as Benny.
- **Psych tracker hooks:** a **named concept or "psychologists found" claim in the first words** → explanation → validation ("this is why you…"). Comment bait is implied by identity.
- **Finance titles:** contrast ("same income, rich or broke"), validation ("you're not behind") and specific dollar figures ("$100k").

## What they do differently (vs. Benny test #1 "$1 Jar")
- **A concept or number in the first 3 words** ("The Darvo method.", "Psychologists found…", "$100k"). The $1 Jar opened on a static title card.
- **The viewer is the subject** ("if a guy is in love with you", "you're not behind", "people who…"). The jar was about a jar.
- **One question, ended at the answer** (Zack D.): no drifting moral at the end.
- **A named mechanism gives authority** (learned helplessness, DARVO, truth stacking). Benny should name the real effect (e.g. "loss aversion") once.
- **Validation/identity titles outperform** in psych (100× outliers for "People Who…"). Finance outliers use **contrast + specific dollars**.
- **Text-only/silent Shorts still hit 2–12M**, so the first frame must read with sound off. Burn the hook in as on-screen text from frame 1.
- **Stick figures already work in this lane** (BillionInspire, 4.5M), which validates Benny's format. Differentiation comes from Benny's face and expressions plus real numbers.

## 5 Benny hook candidates

### 1. ★ CHOSEN: "Heads, you win $150. Tails, you lose $100. Do you flip?"
- **First frame:** Benny (Psychology outfit) holds a giant coin up to the camera. On-screen: "+$150 / −$100". Spoken by 0.1 s.
- **Expression:** "hmm" (one brow up) → shocked → smug "alam ko na".
- **Open loop:** "Most people say no. The math says yes. One number in your brain decides."
- **Payoff:** losses feel about **2×** a win (meta-analysis mean 1.955). $100 lost feels like about −$195, so the $150 win "isn't enough". Yet the average flip is **+$25**, and over 100 flips you end up down only **1.76%** of the time.
- **Ending:** choice/loop: "So… how much would YOU need to flip? Comment your number." Or loop straight back to "Heads, $150…".
- **Pattern inspired by:** Zack D. Films (one question in 1 s, end at the answer) plus the psych-tracker "named concept" hooks.
- **Sources:** https://www.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jel.20221698 · https://litcharts.com/lit/thinking-fast-and-slow/part-4-chapter-26 · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_aversion · https://atticusli.com/replication-crisis/loss-aversion/ (caveats)

### 2. "If you pay with a card, your brain lets you pay double."
- **First frame:** Money Benny with cash in one hand and a card in the other; a bar chart where the card bar is 2× taller.
- **Open loop:** "One experiment with basketball tickets showed how much more."
- **Payoff:** in an MIT study (Prelec & Simester 2001), card buyers were willing to pay over 100% more for the same tickets.
- **Ending:** "Cash or card this week? A or B."
- **Inspired by:** finance "money mistake with a $ cost" titles (Humphrey Yang "10 Things That Are No Longer Worth Your Money").
- **Sources:** https://news.mit.edu/news-clip/bloomberg-196 (over 100% more, secondary). **The paper itself (DOI 10.1023/a:1008196717017) was not opened. Verify the exact figure before using it.**

### 3. "Your focus breaks every 47 seconds. It used to be 2½ minutes."
- **First frame:** Night Benny with a phone and a stopwatch counting 0:47.
- **Open loop:** "And getting it back takes way longer."
- **Payoff:** Gloria Mark measured about 47 s on any screen before switching, versus about 2.5 min when measurement started.
- **Ending:** loop: "You made it past 47 seconds? Prove it in the comments."
- **Inspired by:** psych "named finding" hooks ("Psychologists found…").
- **Sources:** https://texasstandard.org/stories/attention-span-book-gloria-mark/ (47 s, 2½ min). The APA transcript page loaded empty. **The ~25-minute refocus figure is unverified, so do not use it.**

### 4. "If you're under 35 and feel broke, look at this number."
- **First frame:** Everyday Benny looking at a bank app, then a big "median" number.
- **Payoff:** median net worth for under-35 households. **Sources conflict:** Boldin says $32,800 (2025 SCF, 2025 dollars); others say about $39,000 (2022 SCF). **Not usable until it is checked on federalreserve.gov.**
- **Inspired by:** Humphrey Yang "You're Not Behind…" (8.6×).
- **Sources:** https://www.boldin.com/retirement/average-net-worth-by-age/

### 5. "$1 million now, or 1 penny doubled for 30 days? Pick fast."
- **First frame:** Captain Benny between a cash stack and a single penny. "A or B?"
- **Open loop:** "On day 20 the penny looks like a terrible choice."
- **Payoff (my math):** day 20 = $5,242.88, day 30 = $5,368,709.12 (0.01 × 2^29). The total across all 30 days = $10,737,418.23.
- **Ending:** A/B choice.
- **Note:** this is pure math (no source needed beyond the computation), but it is in the same "compounding reveal" family as the rejected $1 Jar, so it is lower priority.

## Why #1
- (a) A specific, verifiable fact (meta-analysis of 607 estimates, mean 1.955) plus a counter-intuitive math reveal (+$25 per flip; down only 1.76% of the time over 100 flips).
- (b) Trivial to stage: a coin, +/− labels, two bars ("feels like" vs "real"), a counter.
- (c) A second-person question in the first second, about **your** brain. It is interactive, and the answer is personal.
- (d) It is nothing like the $1 Jar: no saving or accumulation, it is a decision, and it has a built-in A/B ending.

## Math check (python, `.venv/Scripts/python`)
- EV per flip = 0.5×150 − 0.5×100 = **+$25**
- Felt value with λ = 1.955: 75 − 97.75 = **−$22.75**. Break-even win = **$195.50** (λ range 1.820–2.102 gives $182.00–$210.20)
- P(net loss) over n flips: 1 flip 50%, 10 flips **17.19%**, 100 flips **1.76%** (a loss requires < 40 heads)
- Penny: 0.01×2^19 = $5,242.88. 0.01×2^29 = $5,368,709.12. (2^30−1)/100 = $10,737,418.23
