# Sample scripts with the new hook / logic / twist method (2026-11-25)

These are text drafts for the owner to judge. There is no voice and no render yet, and the hook rotation did not advance. Each is about 35–40 s.

## A. "The Walkie-Talkie From 1995"

**Hook text:** A KID ANSWERED FROM 1995 · **Set:** bedroom (night), house · **Clue:** line 6 · **Twist:** line 9

1. My dad gave me an old walkie-talkie instead of a phone. That night, a kid answered.
2. He said his name was Noel, and that it was 1995.
3. I laughed. But he had never heard of a phone.
4. So every night we talked. He was terrible at math, so I helped him.
5. Then one night, Noel said a big kid took his lunch every single day.
6. I told him what my dad always tells me. Stand tall. Look him in the eye. Say no.
7. The next night, he didn't answer. Or the night after that.
8. I ran to tell Dad the walkie-talkie was broken.
9. He was holding an old photo. A boy with a walkie-talkie. On the back: Noel, 1995.
10. Dad smiled. Thanks for the math help. And for the advice.

*Why it works:*
- The hook is a concrete impossible fact plus an open question: who is the kid?
- The clue in line 6 looks like normal advice. After the twist, the viewer realises Dad learned it from his own kid, so the story loops in time.
- The ending is a feeling, not a lesson.

## B. "The Team With Nobody On It"

**Hook text:** WHY IS THAT GOAL EMPTY? · **Set:** field, street · **Clue:** line 3 · **Twist:** lines 5–7

1. Every day at recess, Leo picks the same team. The one with nobody on it.
2. He plays soccer alone against the whole class. And he loses. Every single day.
3. When Rex asked why, Leo pointed at the empty goal. That spot is taken.
4. For a whole year, nobody stood in that goal. Leo wouldn't let anyone.
5. Then one Monday, a boy in a wheelchair rolled onto the field.
6. Leo dropped the ball and ran. Ollie! You're back!
7. Ollie was his goalie, before the accident. Leo had promised to keep his spot.
8. Rex looked at Ollie. Then at the scoreboard. Two hundred losses.
9. Then Rex walked over and stood on Leo's side. So did everyone else.
10. That day, Leo's team finally won. Ollie didn't let in a single goal.

*Why it works:*
- The hook is a strange choice the viewer needs explained.
- "That spot is taken" is the planted clue, and the twist turns "the kid who always loses" into "the most loyal kid in school".
- Kenney's rig already includes wheelchair-sit and wheelchair-move clips.

## C. "The Vegetable War"

**Hook text:** THEY'RE PAYING ME TO FIGHT · **Set:** house (evening) · **Clue:** line 8 · **Twist:** lines 6–9

1. Grandma pays me a dollar for every vegetable I eat. Grandpa pays me two dollars not to.
2. So every dinner is a war.
3. Grandma hides broccoli in my pasta. Grandpa slips me cash under the table.
4. By Friday, I had forty dollars and a very confused stomach.
5. Then I found their secret notebook under the cookie jar.
6. Two columns. Grandma's said: he came. Grandpa's said: he came.
7. Every single night had two check marks.
8. Before the vegetable war, I only visited on holidays.
9. They never cared about the vegetables. They just wanted me at their table.
10. So tonight I ate the broccoli. And I gave Grandpa his money back.
11. He leaned in and whispered, Same time tomorrow? Three dollars.

*Why it works:*
- The hook is a funny, impossible-sounding deal.
- The twist reframes the "war" as a plan by two lonely grandparents.
- The last line is a laugh, not a lesson, and it loops back to the deal in line 1.
