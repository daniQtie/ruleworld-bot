## Core expressions

| Expression | Brows | Eyes | Mouth | Body reaction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neutral | Relaxed | Normal focus | Closed/resting | Still, relaxed |
| Happy | Slightly raised | Soft/squinting | Natural smile | Open shoulders, small lean |
| Sad | Inner corners raised | Downward gaze | Corners down | Head lowers, shoulders soften |
| Angry | Angled inward/down | Focused | Pressed/frown | Jaw set, restrained tension |
| Scared | Raised | Wide, darting | Slightly open | Pull back, tense shoulders |
| Surprised | High | Wide, fixed | Round/open | Brief freeze, head recoil |
| Confused | One brow raised | Side glance | Uneven | Head tilt |
| Crying | Inner brows raised | Wet/teary, blink | Trembling lower lip | Shoulders contract, breath uneven |
| Relieved | Relaxed | Soft | Gentle smile | Exhale, shoulders release |

## Additional acting states

- Suspicious: one brow slightly down, eyes narrow, head still.
- Embarrassed: gaze down and away, small tight smile, shoulders tuck.
- Guilty: eyes avoid contact, brows rise inward, mouth tightens.
- Determined: focused eyes, stable brows, jaw firm, upright posture.
- Shocked: brief freeze before reaction; avoid permanently wide eyes.
- Trying not to cry: eyes glisten, lower eyelids tense, mouth held still.
- Nervous smile: smile is small and uneven; eyes do not fully smile.
- Comforting: soft gaze, relaxed brows, gentle forward lean.

## Reaction timing

- Establish the stimulus first, then show the face react.
- Hold important emotional expressions long enough to read; do not swap expressions every few frames.
- Use subtle transitions, not abrupt face replacement.
- Give the character a gaze target (another character, message, monster, empty chair).
- Avoid random blinking during every important syllable.
- Use eye direction to show what the character notices or avoids.
- Match body movement to facial emotion.

## Lip-sync guide

If the tool supports lip sync or mouth shapes:
- Match mouth openings to syllabic rhythm, not every letter.
- Keep mouth movement small during quiet speech.
- Pause mouth movement when the character listens.
- Do not leave the mouth smiling while the character says a sad or frightening line.
- For non-speaking characters, use listening reactions rather than continuous mouth movement.

## Scene 001 examples

- Lily sees the monster: eyes shift over Noah's shoulder first; brows rise; lips part; body leans away.
- Noah says “Of course”: a small reassuring smile, but a slight gaze break reveals discomfort.
- Birthday scene: Lily looks at the empty chair, then at Noah; eyes glisten; voice and face remain restrained.
- Noah confesses: his smile disappears; gaze lowers; brows draw inward; shoulders relax only after the truth is spoken.
- Lily forgives him: tears remain, but the brows soften and a small genuine smile appears.
