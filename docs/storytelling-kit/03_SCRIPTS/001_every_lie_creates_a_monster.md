# Script 001 — Every Lie Creates a Monster

**Working title:** In This Town, Only the Person You Lie To Can See Your Monster...  
**Format:** Vertical YouTube Short  
**Target duration:** 2:15–2:25  
**Genre:** Emotional fantasy / suspense  
**Language:** Natural American English  
**Main characters:** Noah (15), Lily (8), the Lie-Monster  
**Core emotional idea:** A protective lie can still make someone feel alone.

## World rule

Every lie creates a creature. The person being lied to can see it, but the liar cannot. The creature grows with every lie. Telling the truth makes it disappear.

## Scene 1 — Hook (0:00–0:08)

**Visual:** Close on Lily's face. Her eyes are fixed on something behind Noah. Over his shoulder, a small shadow creature with dim eyes is visible. Noah cannot see it.

**Narrator — quiet, urgent, suspenseful:**

“In our town, the person you lie to is the only one who can see the monster. And my little sister watched mine grow for three days.”

## Scene 2 — The rule (0:08–0:22)

**Visual:** Brief visual examples: a neighbor tells a lie; a small shadow appears behind the neighbor, visible only to the listener. Keep this fast and understandable.

**Narrator — clear, mysterious, controlled:**

“Every lie creates a creature. The liar can't see it. Only the person hearing the lie can. And there's only one way to make it disappear. Tell the truth.”

## Scene 3 — The real problem (0:22–0:43)

**Visual:** Noah sits alone in the kitchen, looking at his phone. A message from Dad reads: “I'm sorry. I can't make it this weekend.” Lily enters holding a handmade paper crown.

**Lily — hopeful, excited:**

“Do you think Dad will come early for my birthday?”

**Noah — trying to sound cheerful, hiding sadness:**

“Of course. He promised.”

**Visual:** A tiny black creature crawls out from behind Noah's shoe. Lily notices it. Noah doesn't.

## Scene 4 — The first warning (0:43–1:00)

**Visual:** Lily stops smiling and looks behind her brother.

**Lily — confused, frightened, almost whispering:**

“Noah... what's standing behind you?”

**Noah — dismissive, pretending nothing is wrong:**

“Nothing. You're probably just tired. Go finish your crown.”

**Visual:** The creature grows slightly. Lily takes one step away.

## Scene 5 — Escalation (1:00–1:27)

**Visual:** Three short moments across different locations. Each lie makes the creature larger.

**At breakfast — Lily:**

“Did Dad remember my favorite cake?”

**Noah:**

“Of course. Chocolate, right?”

**At school — Lily shows her drawing:**

“Do you think he'll like this?”

**Noah:**

“He's going to love it.”

**That night — Lily looks at the window:**

“Will he really be here tomorrow?”

**Noah — slightly impatient, then softer:**

“Yes, Lily. I already told you.”

**Visual:** By the final moment, the creature is taller than Noah. Noah still cannot see it.

## Scene 6 — The birthday (1:27–1:49)

**Visual:** Birthday table. Two children and an empty chair. An untouched plate is waiting for Dad. Lily holds the cake lighter. The monster fills much of the hallway behind Noah.

**Lily — quiet, trying not to cry:**

“Should we wait a little longer?”

**Noah:** Opens his mouth but cannot answer.

**Lily — looking toward the monster:**

“Noah... I think it gets bigger every time you tell me he's coming.”

**Narrator — lower volume, slower, tense:**

“That's when I realized... she had seen every lie.”

## Scene 7 — The confession (1:49–2:10)

**Visual:** Close-up on Noah. His fake smile disappears. His eyes lower. His hands tremble slightly.

**Noah — honest, guilty, almost breaking:**

“Dad isn't coming, Lily. He messaged me yesterday. I was scared you'd be heartbroken. I thought if I kept saying yes, tomorrow wouldn't hurt.”

**Visual:** The creature cracks. Its eyes dim. It slowly dissolves into tiny particles.

## Scene 8 — The payoff (2:10–2:25)

**Visual:** Lily wipes her eyes and takes Noah's hand.

**Lily — soft, vulnerable, sincere:**

“I was already sad, Noah. I just didn't want to be sad alone.”

She places the handmade paper crown on his head.

**Lily — small smile:**

“Can you stay for the dance?”

**Noah — warm, gentle:**

“Of course. And this time, I mean it.”

**Final visual:** They dance beside the birthday cake. The empty chair remains, but the monster is gone.

**Narrator — warm, reflective, gentle pause:**

“Sometimes, the truth hurts for a moment. But a lie can make someone feel alone for much longer.”

**END.**

## Production notes

- The emotional payoff is a quiet reveal, not a loud jump scare.
- The monster is a visual metaphor. Keep its design simple enough to remain consistent.
- Do not show Dad physically. His absence is communicated through the phone message and empty chair.
- Do not over-explain the rule after the opening.
- Keep the scene pacing tight. If the generated narration runs longer than 2:25, shorten pauses or dialogue without removing the emotional turning points.
- This is an original sample draft. Claude should run story QA and may suggest improvements, but should not replace the premise with a generic twist.
